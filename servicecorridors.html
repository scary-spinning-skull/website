The hull of the Warden is not solid, but honeycombed with many tunnels and chambers. A common type of tunnel is the service corridor, an X-shaped configuration of tunnels which were intended to offer access to lengthy components of the ship which can't be moved. Each tunnel is 20' high and wide, and the floors, walls, and ceilings are covered with a bewildering array of wires, pipes, and boxes covered with blinking lights. These corridors gently curve around the decks they're found on, although the curvature is so slight as to be unnoticeable to passengers traveling through them. There is almost always a 5' wide catwalk suspended from the ceiling with steel cables, and anchor points for engineers' grapples are scattered across every other surface seemingly at random.

Signs of other scavengers can sometimes be found. Security bots (MCC p. 150) have a 5% chance of patrolling a tunnel once every five hours, and a construction bot without bulldozer or crane components (MCC p. 149) has a 5% chance of sweeping through on a routine maintenance check every hour. One of the former will begin seeking the PCs out and find them within an hour if they cause any damage to the devices on the walls (they also have a 50% chance of exposing themselves to Level II radiation if they break any box-like components). A construction bot will attend to the damage in six hours.

These corridors connect to each other as they run through the hull, sometimes opening back onto a deck, and, rarely, offering characters a way to access the ship's exterior. To determine where and at what angle, simply roll on the table below for every five minutes of game time spent walking. If bodies are found in these tunnels, they will have a 25% chance of having a gray maintenance wristband (1-5 on a d20) and a 10% chance of having a science wristband (6-7 on a d20).
